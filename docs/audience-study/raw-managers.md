# Audience study: raw blocks, segment `managers`

40 synthetic visitors: operations, HR, admin, sales/CRM and store/ecommerce managers at growing SMEs (30–500 staff)
who research and shortlist vendors before the owner decides. 28 India, 12 abroad (UAE 4, Singapore 3, Malaysia 2,
UK 3). 16 phone, 24 desktop. 13 marked sceptical or busy (`[S]`).

Each block ends with a `Codes` line used for tallying. Black-box codes: COST, TIME (how long until live), TEAM (who
are these people), ADOPT (will my team use it / training), AFTER (what happens after the form), DATA (hosting,
security, certifications), LOCAL (support in my country/time zone), REF (cannot verify clients), RUNCOST (ongoing
hosting/support cost). Missing codes: PRICE (any number or range), TIMELINE, DEMO (real screens, video, demo),
NAMES (client names, testimonials, references), TEAMPAGE, MYTOOLS (Tally, Zoho, biometrics, GST etc. named),
ONEPAGER (something to forward: PDF, deck, summary), BOOKING (calendar), WHATSAPP, TRAINING (onboarding/rollout
plan), CERTS, OFFICE (local office), SECTORCASE (a case in my sector), FIELD (mobile/offline field app).

### MGR-01 · Operations head · auto-components manufacturer, 220 staff · Chennai (Ambattur) · 40–49 · tech med
Desktop, office broadband. Trigger: purchase orders approved on WhatsApp, nobody can find them at audit. Arrived via
Google "custom ERP software company Chennai".
- **First 10 s:** "Custom software, Chennai, since 2012. Systems that grow with you." Gets it from the hero sub line
  about outgrowing spreadsheets. Stays.
- **Path:** Hero → weave → story → services → systems explorer (stops on Operations & supply chain, reads the
  Procurement funnel) → clicks "Explore Operations" → `/systems/operations` → "Purchase orders get approved on
  WhatsApp and nobody can find them later" → impact list → back to Pricing → Contact.
- **Reaction:** The operations page reads like his own Monday meeting. Likes "Every PO has an approver, a date and a
  trail". Finds the 3D loom pretty but irrelevant. Annoyed there is no idea of cost or how long a procurement module
  takes. Rating **8**.
- **Black box:** Cost of a first module; how many weeks before his buyers use it.
- **Expected but missing:** A price range; a typical timeline; a screenshot of a real PO screen.
- **Outcome:** **Contact now** (calls the Chennai number, a local office is reassuring). Would have been faster with a
  "typical first module: X weeks" line.
- Codes: BB COST, TIME · MISS PRICE, TIMELINE, DEMO

### MGR-02 · HR manager · IT services firm, 140 staff · Bengaluru · 30–39 · tech med
Phone (mid-range Android, 4G). Trigger: leave and attendance in three places. Arrived via LinkedIn ad.
- **First 10 s:** Preloader "Untangling the threads", then glowing threads. Reads "Custom software for businesses
  that have outgrown spreadsheets". Understands, stays, a bit impatient.
- **Path:** Hero → story (pinned, scrolls a lot for three words) → services → explorer, swipes to People &
  workforce (headcount, payroll ₹18.4L). Looks for an HR page in the menu, none. Footer → `/systems/people`.
- **Reaction:** "Leave requests come in by WhatsApp, email and a sticky note on my desk" made her laugh. But her
  first thought is "we could just buy greytHR or Keka". FAQ line "If an off-the-shelf tool will do the job, we will
  say so" earns trust. Rating **6**.
- **Black box:** Why custom over an HR SaaS; cost compared with per-employee SaaS pricing; whether staff will
  actually install and use an app.
- **Expected but missing:** Comparison with off-the-shelf HR tools; a price range; how rollout/training works.
- **Outcome:** **Bookmark-later**. A "custom vs off-the-shelf" explainer with rough cost would have moved her.
- Codes: BB COST, ADOPT · MISS PRICE, TRAINING
